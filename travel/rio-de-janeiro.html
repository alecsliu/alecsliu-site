Rio de Janeiro

I stayed in Leblon from February to April 2026. One thing I've learned through my travels is that few cities have as distinct a feeling, or as strong a sense of place, as Rio. From watching Imperatriz parade through the Sapucaí, to (very many) beach sessions in Leblon and Ipanema, to running around Lagoa, and to watching the Fla-Flu at Maracanã, everything about Rio is magically unforgettable. As the great Tom Jobim once sang, "Estou morrendo de saudade / Rio, teu mar, praias sem fim / Rio, você foi feito pra mim (I'm dying of yearning / Rio, your sea and endless beaches / Rio, you were made for me)"

Restaurants

  • SanLeblon

    An incredibly popular restaurant in the heart of Leblon. The lunch menu (menu executivo) is extremely reasonable for the quality and quantity of sushi, and it was one of my favorite places to take visitors.

  • Ferro e FarinhaLeblon

    This Brazilian pizza chain has locations across the city, with the Ipanema one being the snazziest. I prefer the location on top of Shopping Leblon because it tends to have the shortest lines and was closest to me. It's also particularly great if you have vegetarian or vegan friends, because the coconut curry pizza is legitimately one of the best innovative takes on pizza I've ever had (and almost all their pizzas are fantastic). The drinks here are great too.

  • Giuseppe Grill

    My favorite place to get a nice steak in Rio. Service can be a bit slow, so I wouldn't plan around it, but the atmosphere is good and the food is excellent. They also sell custom plates for their specialty dishes as souvenirs (I wish I'd bought one).

  • Braseiro da GáveaGávea

    There's a reason it's popular. It's worth a short wait, but I wouldn't wait longer than 15–20 minutes, and I didn't think the chicken was all that special.

Hikes & activities

  • Morro Dois IrmãosVidigal

    While this hike does involve going through a favela, the entire process is very streamlined and simple. Moto-taxis will pick you up at a standard price, and once you're at the start of the park, navigating to the summit is very straightforward. The sunrise hike is worth it (no need for an Airbnb guide), but make sure to bring bug repellent and flashlights. The view from Dois Irmãos is, I believe, one of the most beautiful things you can see on Earth.

  • Pedra BonitaTijuca National Park

    A good alternative, with stunning views of Zona Oeste and Barra da Tijuca. For transportation reasons, we did this hike with a guide, which I would recommend for convenience. It's an easier hike than Dois Irmãos and goes through the national park. In theory, there are wild toucans there, but we didn't see any.

  • Reserva de MarapendiBarra da Tijuca

    The starting point for tours of the lagoon is near Ilha da Gigóia, and it makes for a nice, chill day. Unfortunately, parts of the lagoon (near the banks) are definitely very dirty, but if you're interested in seeing capybaras in the wild, this is one of the best places to go.

Shopping

  • Casa FidalgusIpanema

    This small shop in Ipanema has a lot of unique, locally produced specialty goods (mostly consumables, like spirits). Some great, unique cachaça is sold here that I wouldn't recommend wasting in a caipirinha.

  • Casa TucumCentro

    This small shop is an easy stop after visiting the CCBB and/or Casa Brasil. There are a lot of reasonably priced and interesting goods to be found here, produced by various Indigenous peoples across Brazil. It's easy to miss if you're not looking for it.
