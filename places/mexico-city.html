Mexico City

Everything about Mexico City is on an unimaginable scale. It's a city with amazing food, unique history, and absolutely stunning architecture and museums which make any length of stay, whether it be a day, a week, or a lifetime, unforgettable. When people ask for recommendations, I often say that if you're staying for less than a week, stick to the beaten path because it's all worth doing.

Luis Barragán

It's hard to visit Mexico City and not fall in love with Barragán, and more and more of his works are becoming publicly accessible. These are the ones I've visited in the city:

  • Casa Luis BarragánTacubaya

    The home of the esteemed architect. It's a must-do for anybody with an interest in his works. Casa Ortega is next door, but getting a response/scheduling a visit is difficult.

  • Casa GilardiSan Miguel Chapultepec

    The famous home with a dining room pool. Visiting this house is easier than any of the other Barragán properties, and the tour guide is a son of the owner who is happy to answer any questions you have about the property.

  • Casa PedregalJardines del Pedregal

    El Pedregal was home to many unique homes and was the playground of Mexican architects. Unfortunately, some of those homes, such as the Casa Cueva (by Juan O'Gorman), were destroyed, as protections for 20th-century works typically lag behind and are light. Fortunately, Casa Pedregal by Barragán was preserved, and the building with its pink kitchen and incorporation of volcanic rock is incredibly unique. It's a bit cliché, but it's a must visit if you like Barragán's works.

Outside the city, I've also visited Cuadra San Cristóbal (the stables) in Atizapán and Casa ITESO in Guadalajara.

Museums & sites

  • Fundación Casa Wabi

    Barragán is probably the most famous architect from Mexico, but anybody who visits CDMX will probably also see Kalach's library, the Biblioteca Vasconcelos. However, Casa Wabi Sabino was also designed by him and is a calm oasis in an otherwise very busy city. Well worth a visit for the architecture alone, and the art exhibitions themselves are often fantastic as well.

  • Museo Nacional de la Acuarela (Alfredo Guati Rojo)Coyoacán

    This museum is a very small, quick visit. I wouldn't make a stop for the museum specifically, but the grounds are beautiful, the content is charming, and it's well worth visiting while in the area.

  • Museo TamayoBosque de Chapultepec

    The Tamayo always has incredibly interesting exhibitions and is architecturally distinct (if you like brutalism). The gift shop is also one of my favorite places to find interesting, local brands as well (1/8 Takamura, Kuxan Che, etc.).

  • MODORoma Norte

    This is just a fun museum that is almost certainly a convenient visit for tourists in Mexico City (unless you don't somehow end up visiting Roma Norte at some point). It's best explored by simply visiting.

  • MUACCiudad Universitaria

    If visiting UNAM (or Coyoacán), the MUAC is a great place to spend half a day. The architecture is sharp and distinct, and designed by Teodoro González de León, who also was behind the Tamayo. With its extensive space, there are always interesting exhibitions going on, often combining education on matters such as the environment with more “traditional” contemporary art exhibitions. Similar to the Tamayo, the MUAC is a great place to visit for more unique souvenirs and gifts to take back home.

  • Museo Dolores OlmedoXochimilco

    Rich people used to do things. This place was closed for years before finally reopening in 2026, and is well worth a visit for anybody with an interest in Diego Rivera or Frida Kahlo.

  • Antiguo Colegio de San IldefonsoCentro Histórico

    A building with a long, prestigious history, now repurposed as a museum with stunning Orozco murals and a great gift shop attached. Spaces like these are a reminder of why Mexico City is known as the city of palaces.

  • Museo Banco de MéxicoCentro Histórico

    As the name implies, the Mexican Bank Museum does feature permanent exhibitions about the history of the bank and Mexican finance. Outside of that, there are often beautiful temporary art exhibitions, and the building itself is absolutely beautiful.

  • Museo de Arte Carrillo Gil (MACG)San Ángel

    Quiet and spacious, the MACG in San Ángel makes for a convenient stop for visitors to a variety of attractions within the region (Museo Casa Estudio Diego Rivera y Frida Kahlo, Casa Pedregal, UNAM, and the Coyoacán attractions). The museum itself is affordable, spacious, and features many works from Mexican greats in its permanent collections, including some cubist works from Diego Rivera.

Misc

  • Mexico City Marathon

    Yes, this is my flex, and I'm doing it unashamedly. I ran the Mexico City Marathon and it was a fantastic experience. The altitude will try to murder you, and the organizers have the devilish idea that making you run to Centro with 0 shade or tree coverage is a fantastic way to finish off the marathon (never mind the difficulties of getting a car or connecting with your friends after the race). But you truly do get to see so many iconic sights while running the race, and the crowd support is absolutely phenomenal and heartwarming. I ran San Francisco the year after and was shocked at how poorly organized a race could be, and how little crowd support there was. Mexico City's marathon goes through one of the most historic cities in the world, but what makes it great is the people. 10/10 would do again.
