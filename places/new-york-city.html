New York City

I went to NYU, and while a student there, I always assumed I'd live in NYC for the rest of my life. Turns out, life doesn't work that way, and I actually really do not like seasonal depression, snow, slush in my boots, or the lack of a beach. But NYC will always hold a special place in my heart, and I spent enough money and time shopping and enjoying my time there to have some recs to share.

Shopping

  • Min & MonSoHo

    Bags and fashion should show your personality. Min & Mon is a lot of things, but boring or trend based is definitely not one of them. It's also much more reasonably priced than most competitors in the contemporary space.

Bookstores

  • Yu and Me BooksChinatown

    As a Chinese-American who is always on the hunt for Asian-American books, this place is a gold mine. The staff are also typically very well-versed and able to give great, helpful hints. I'm also a big fan of the corduroy hats (I've got 2), though I've been asked if I work there multiple times while wearing them.

  • McNally JacksonFinancial District

    I love this place. Can't explain why, just do.

Restaurants

  • BerimbauMidtown

    I went here right after living in Rio and can attest that the food is authentic, albeit not at Rio prices (understandably). Regardless, it's a great place to go for delicious Brazilian food when the ticket to Leblon is a bit too expensive.

  • CheLiEast Village

    Good Shanghainese sons and daughters know this is where to bring your haughty parents for a decent meal out. At least, when I've taken my parents here (more than once), nobody has ever said they could cook this better at home. It's also conveniently close to NYU's campus, for those who are lazy.

  • Cafe ChinaMidtown

    My go-to. Very reasonable prices for private rooms, and the food is consistently good. I was not alive for the “golden age” of Shanghai, but the decor makes me nostalgic for what I've never experienced. The drinks here are also fantastic.

  • Izakaya MEWKoreatown

    Sometimes you go out in Ktown and want somewhere cheap that isn't Korean food. This place is fantastic for a pregame or a postgame at reasonable prices.

  • The Musket RoomNolita

    My favourite one Michelin star restaurant in NYC, out of a crowded field of many, many, many awful one Michelin star restaurants (shoutout Café Boulud: my recommendation is if you accidentally include plastic wrapping in the dish you're serving me, please try apologizing and at least remaking the damn dish). My most recent meal was a bit sour (quite literally), but otherwise it's always been a great, reasonably priced, and inventive meal, and a nice treat that you won't feel bad splurging on.
